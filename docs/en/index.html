Crade Cortex

A Pico sends MIDI, a Raspberry Pi sends its state back over the same cable. The LEDs don't show what your foot asked for — they show what the Pi confirmed.

Pico RP2040 · CircuitPython · LCD1602 — Raspberry Pi · PiPedal 2.0.110

Section 1Overview

PiPedal is the single source of truth. The Pico holds no state of its own. It sends a CC and waits. The LED only lights on confirmation.

DirectionTransportContents
Pico → PiUSB MIDI (ALSA)CC 102–105, 110–113
Pi → PicoUSB CDC serialstate, names, tuner
Pico → PiUSB CDC serialtuner mode, state request

Why the feedback loop exists

Without it, the Pico decides and hopes the Pi follows. That holds as long as it's the only thing acting, and it breaks in four cases: at startup, when you touch the web interface from your phone, if pipedald restarts on its own, and on a bank change.

With the feedback, a single state exists. The whole class of desynchronisation bugs disappears by construction.

Tuple ordering. BROCHES_SWITCHES, BROCHES_LEDS and BRILLANCE must stay aligned. BRILLANCE[i] applies to BROCHES_LEDS[i].

LCD1602 wiring

LCD pinTo
1 VSS, 5 R/W, 16 KGND — Pico pin 38
2 VDD, 15 AVBUS — pin 40, 5 V
3 V010 kΩ potentiometer wiper
4 RSGP10
6 EGP11
11–14 D4–D7GP12 → GP15

Pins 7 to 10 stay unconnected: that's 4-bit mode.

The contrast potentiometer is not optional. A floating V0 gives either a solid black display or a blank one — the number one cause of "my LCD doesn't work". Outer legs to 5 V and ground, wiper on V0.

R/W must go to ground. That puts the LCD in write-only mode so it can never drive 5 V back into a Pico input. This is what makes the 5 V / 3.3 V mix safe here.

The backlight resistor is already on the board — R8, marked 101 for 100 Ω — on QAPASS 1602A modules. Pin 15 therefore goes straight to 5 V. Check on any other model.

The HD44780 runs on 5 V but reads 3.3 V outputs. Officially marginal, since its high threshold is 3.5 V. In practice it works. If the display turns erratic, that's where to look.

Ground

Star topology: one short common wire soldered to the Pi's ground, with each switch and LED ground arriving independently. Better fault isolation than daisy-chaining. No audio signal passes through the enclosure, so ground loops don't arise.

Section 4The Pico

Four files at the root of the CIRCUITPY drive, plus two libraries.

Prerequisites. adafruit_midi/ and adafruit_character_lcd/ must be in /lib, from the official CircuitPython bundle matching your major version. Without them the imports fail and the pedal doesn't start at all.

boot.py

usb_midi.enable()
usb_cdc.enable(console=True, data=True)
supervisor.set_usb_identification(product="Pedalier")
usb_midi.set_names(streaming_interface_name="Switches")

RP2040 endpoint budget: 7 usable pairs. CIRCUITPY (1) + console (2) + data (2) + MIDI (1) = 6, one pair of headroom. If it drops into safe mode, set console=False.

Hard reset required. boot.py is only re-read at power-up. Saving the file isn't enough. Its print() output goes to boot_out.txt, never to the console.

Section 5Display

Startup

+----------------+ | CRADE CORTEX | |########## | +----------------+

Letters appear one at a time, in their final position. No continuous motion: a liquid-crystal display takes 150 to 250 ms to flip a pixel, and any sliding turns to mush. A fixed reveal stays crisp because no crystal has to travel back.

The bar uses five custom CGRAM characters — one, two, three, four, five columns of pixels — giving 80 steps instead of 16. CGRAM slot 0 is left free: chr(0) terminates a Python string and would break the write.

It doesn't lie. It climbs to 94 %, then stops and pulses in place until the Pi says something. Booting without the daemon is immediately visible: the bar pulses indefinitely.

T_LETTRE = 0.1                       # one letter appearing
T_TITRE  = T_LETTRE * len(TITRE)     # derived
T_PAUSE  = 0.25                      # dead time before the bar
T_BARRE  = 1.60                      # fill up to the plateau
T_FINAL  = 0.35                      # hold at full

Below 0.06 s per letter the smearing returns. That's the panel's physical limit, not a code defect.

Normal

+----------------+ |Fender Clean | |2 Chorus | +----------------+

Line 1: pedalboard name over 15 characters, plus a link indicator in column 16. Line 2: number and name of the active snapshot. A ! at the end of line 1 flags a lost heartbeat.

Tuner

+----------------+ | E2 -12c | |.......|#|......| +----------------+

The # marker moves, with the centre reference on columns 7 and 8. Within 4 cents, -12c becomes OK. If the pedalboard has no TooB Tuner, the display reads absent du pdb.

Section 6Serial protocol

One ASCII line per event, terminated by \n. Bidirectional.

Pi → Pico

M:<text>Active snapshot name
T:<note>:<cents>Tuner, e.g. T:E2:-12
T:-Tuner, no signal
T:!No tuner in the pedalboard

Pico → Pi

FrameMeaning
A1 / A0Enter / leave tuner mode

Any received frame counts as a sign of life, not just HB.

The safeguards

SafeguardDelayBehaviour
Optimistic fallback200 msNo confirmation: the LED lights anyway. Degraded but playable.
Heartbeat3 sThree missed beats: slow blink and a ! on screen.
Load wait2.5 sOn F1/F2, the LED stays lit until the new preset name arrives.
? beacon1 HzRequests state until the Pi speaks up.

Loading a NAM model takes one to two seconds: a simple flash would be over before anything moved. And without the first two safeguards, a dead link leaves the LEDs frozen on the last value received — you'd be playing blind without knowing it.

Section 7Resynchronisation

The trap. The daemon caches state and only emits on change. When the Pico restarts, nothing has changed on PiPedal's side: it receives only HB and stays blind. Worse, a partial change such as a snapshot sends only S and M, not N:, leaving line 1 empty.

Two independent mechanisms, because they cover different cases.

On the Pi. LienPico.reconnecte() flags a serial port reopening; the daemon then pushes the whole state back. Covers physical unplugging and hard resets.

On the Pico. While nothing has arrived, the Pico emits ? once a second and the daemon answers by pushing state. Covers what the first mechanism misses: saving code.py, where the Pico restarts without the port always disappearing on the Linux side.

The beacon stops by itself on the first received frame.

Section 8The Pi

The daemon

sudo apt install python3-serial
pip3 install websockets --break-system-packages
python3 pipedal_bridge.py -v

It subscribes to the websocket on localhost, caches state and only emits on change. Reconnection uses exponential backoff, and it survives the serial port disappearing.

udev rule

Without it, the ttyACM number can change with USB enumeration order. When multiple CDC/ACM interfaces are present, a fixed path such as /dev/ttyACM0 is therefore not reliable; the rule below creates a stable name for the pedal.

# /etc/udev/rules.d/99-pipedal-pico.rules
SUBSYSTEM=="tty", ATTRS{idVendor}=="239a", ATTRS{product}=="Pedalier", \
  ENV{ID_USB_INTERFACE_NUM}=="02", SYMLINK+="pipedal-pico"
sudo udevadm control --reload && sudo udevadm trigger
ls -l /dev/pipedal-pico

Interface 02 is the data port. The console carries a different number.

systemd service

# /etc/systemd/system/pipedal-bridge.service
[Unit]
Description=PiPedal to Pico bridge
After=pipedald.service
Wants=pipedald.service

[Service]
Type=simple
User=pi
WorkingDirectory=/opt/crade-cortex
ExecStart=/usr/bin/python3 /opt/crade-cortex/pipedal_bridge.py
Restart=always
RestartSec=2

[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target
sudo systemctl daemon-reload
sudo systemctl enable --now pipedal-bridge
journalctl -u pipedal-bridge -f

The Python path must be the venv's if websockets lives there rather than system-wide. And After= orders startup without guaranteeing the websocket is ready: the daemon will fail once or twice at boot and then reconnect. That's normal.

groups | grep dialout || sudo usermod -aG dialout $USER

Stop the service before running the daemon by hand: two instances would fight over the serial port.

Section 9Websocket protocol

Internal, undocumented API. Reverse-engineered by capture on 2.0.110. It will change between versions — re-verify after every PiPedal update.

Endpoint: ws://127.0.0.1:80/pipedal. Full reference in PROTOCOL.md.

Envelope

// request with no argument
[{"message": "hello", "replyTo": 2}]

// request with an argument
[{"message": "monitorPort", "replyTo": 24},
 {"instanceId": 16, "key": "FREQ", "updateRate": 0.05}]

// server response
[{"reply": 2, "message": "ehlo"}, 20]

The handshake

The client must send hello on connect. Without it the server never registers it as a recipient and broadcasts nothing.

This caused the daemon's first failure: it connected, retrieved the initial state correctly, then stayed silent forever.

Acknowledgements

When a pushed message's header carries replyTo, the client must answer. This is flow control.

[{"reply": 996, "message": "onVuUpdate"}, true]

Snapshot or preset change

Broadcast to every connected client, including the one that caused it. That's what makes confirmation work when the change comes from your foot.

[{"message": "onPedalboardChanged"},
 {"clientId": -1,
  "pedalboard": {
    "name": "Fender Clean",
    "selectedSnapshot": 1,        // ZERO-BASED
    "items": [{"instanceId": 16, "uri": "…toob-tuner"}, …],
    "snapshots": [{"name": "Default", "color": "purple"}, …]
  }}]

The index to relay is body.pedalboard.selectedSnapshot, zero-based. The serial protocol is 1-based, hence the +1 in the daemon.

Volume. Each broadcast is around 9 kB: PiPedal resends the whole pedalboard, not just the index. Harmless at a few changes a minute, but max_size must be raised on the connection.

Tuner

[{"message": "monitorPort", "replyTo": 24},
 {"instanceId": 16, "key": "FREQ", "updateRate": 0.05}]
// the response carries the subscription handle
[{"reply": 24, "message": "monitorPort"}, 29]

// then, periodically
[{"replyTo": 2, "message": "onMonitorPortOutput"},
 {"subscriptionHandle": 29, "value": 40.12}]

// unsubscribe
[{"message": "unmonitorPort"}, 29]

The TooB Tuner's FREQ port gives a floating-point MIDI note number, -1 when there is no signal. Integer part: the note. Decimal: the deviation in cents.

The instanceId is not hard-coded. It happens to be 16 in the current pedalboard but changes with the preset. The daemon finds it by matching the toob-tuner URI in items, and re-subscribes when it moves.

The daemon quantises cents in steps of 2 and emits no more than 11 frames per second. A tuner naturally jitters on the last cent: without this the display would shimmer constantly and block the Pico. The subscription exists only during tuner mode, on the Pico's A1 command.

Section 10Known pitfalls

Stray binding on CC 102

The TooB Tuner carries a __bypass binding on CC 102, duplicating snapshot1. One press triggers both. Remove it in PiPedal.

Daemon cache

It only emits on change. After a Pico restart, without the mechanisms in section 7, the pedal stays blind.

DTR

usb_cdc.data.read() returns nothing until the host asserts DTR. pyserial does it on open; a shell printf does not. A redirection test can therefore fail on a perfectly good port.

LCD response time

150 to 250 ms per pixel. Any continuously moving animation blurs. Fixed reveals, and bars that only ever add pixels, stay crisp.

Pico USB restart

Every save of code.py makes the serial port disappear and come back. The daemon must absorb that — it does.

Pin ordering

Swapping two entries between BROCHES_LEDS and BRILLANCE is invisible while all LEDs are the same colour, and painful to diagnose afterwards.

SSH sessions

A daemon launched in the foreground dies with the session. Use tmux for debugging, systemd for real.

Section 11Bring-up

Each step validates the previous one.

  1. adafruit_midi/ and adafruit_character_lcd/ copied into /lib on CIRCUITPY.
  2. boot.py in place, hard reset. ls /dev/ttyACM* should show two ports.
  3. udev rule installed. ls -l /dev/pipedal-pico should point to one of them.
  4. Contrast set: black blocks on power-up prove the LCD is alive. Turn until they fade.
  5. printf 'S3\n' > /dev/pipedal-pico — LED 3 lights. The whole USB chain is validated without a line of websocket.
  6. printf 'N:Test\nS2\nM:Chorus\n' > /dev/pipedal-pico — the display shows it.
  7. python3 pipedal_bridge.py -v — the bar fills and the display switches to normal mode.
  8. Change a snapshot from the web interface — LED and display follow.
  9. Press F3 — tuner mode, and Accordeur : abonnement sur instance 16 in the logs.
